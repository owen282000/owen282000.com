Common questions

Do I need Home Assistant?

No. Home Assistant gets the most out of it, with the integration's sensors and history, but both apps also send to the Grafana stack, to any MQTT broker, and to any URL that accepts a JSON POST, such as n8n, Node-RED or a script of your own. The webhook guide shows the payload.

Can I ask an AI about my data?

Yes, with a model you choose, once the data is on your server. The apps contain no AI and never talk to a model. The Grafana stack has a read-only MCP server, and in Home Assistant a community server, ha-mcp, reads the history. With a local model nothing leaves your network; with a cloud model such as Claude, your questions and the results go to its provider. Health Connect data over MCP has the steps.

Why a separate app instead of the Home Assistant companion app?

Keep the companion app for presence, notifications and device sensors; the two run side by side. Its 36 Health Connect sensors show the latest value or a daily total, with history from the day you enable them. Life Dashboard Companion reads 34 types, keeps every day in long-term statistics, adds screen time per app, and also sends to MQTT and webhooks. The comparison lists the differences.

Which watches, rings and scales work?

Anything that writes to Health Connect or Apple Health: Samsung Health, Apple Watch, Google Health (Fitbit and Pixel Watch), Garmin Connect, Oura, Withings, Gadgetbridge and many more. What arrives depends on what each vendor shares: Garmin and Samsung, for example, don't share HRV or resting heart rate with Health Connect, and Garmin Connect needs Android 14. The brand guides show what each one writes and what it keeps to itself.

What does it cost?

The code of all four parts is MIT licensed and free. On Android, Google Play is the easy way: €4.99 once (US$4.99), no subscription, with automatic updates, and buying it supports development. The free routes stay free: the signed APK on GitHub, Obtainium, which installs that same APK, and F-Droid. All of them are the same app, built from the same source and signed with the same key. The iPhone app works the same way: building it yourself with Xcode is free, and the App Store version is the paid convenience, €6.99 once (US$5.99), with no subscription.

What do these words mean?

Health Connect is the place on Android where Samsung Health, Garmin Connect and other apps store your health data; Apple Health is the same on iPhone. Home Assistant is open-source software that runs a smart home on a computer in your house. The Marketplace (HACS before Home Assistant 2026.11) is the store for extra Home Assistant integrations. An APK is an Android app file you install yourself, outside the Play Store. A webhook is a web address on your own server that receives data. MQTT is a messaging system many smart homes use. A backfill sends older data, as far back as your phone has it.

Is it a medical device?

No. Life Dashboard Companion moves data your watch, ring or scale already recorded to a server you run. It doesn't measure, diagnose or treat anything and gives no health advice. Talk to a doctor about what the numbers mean for you.

How is it built, and where does AI come in?

I build it with a lot of help from Claude Code, which drafted a large part of the code. I decide what gets built and read every change before it lands. CI runs unit tests, lint and an instrumented suite on an Android emulator, and changes to syncing are tested by hand on a real phone against Home Assistant before a release. The apps contain no AI and call no AI service. The AI policy has the details.

It's one person's project. What if it stops?

Your setup doesn't depend on that person. There's no Life Dashboard server to switch off, your history lives in your own Home Assistant or database, and the MIT license lets anyone carry the code on. The first release shipped in February 2026, and the changelog tracks every change since.
